Curb Cut: an Agentforce agent that's on the worker's side.

PROBLEM TO SOLVE

Thirty in a hundred white-collar workers have a disability. Three tell their employer. The rest do the maths: what would it cost to ask for the chair, the lamp, the hour, and what would my manager think of me on Monday? Most decide it isn't worth it and carry on hurting quietly.

The form is the barrier. To get help you must find the policy, learn its words, hold a work login, and explain your body to the person who decides what you're worth. For a contractor, a temp, someone who is Deaf, someone whose first language isn't English, or someone with a condition nobody can see, that form is a door that doesn't open.

The money was never the barrier: 61% of the 1,425 employers who gave the Job Accommodation Network cost figures through 2024 said the accommodation cost them nothing. The asking was.

OUR SOLUTION

Curb Cut turns asking into one sentence. You say what's hard, in your own words, and you never have to say why. It shows you what other people in your situation have asked for, from a library of real accommodations, each with its source and usual cost. Knowing is allowed, and asking is optional. If you do want to ask, it writes the request in your words, reads it back, and sends nothing until you clearly say yes.

There's no field for a diagnosis anywhere. It isn't encrypted or hidden behind a permission. It simply isn't there, and a build check fails if anyone adds one. Name a condition anyway and it's stripped before anything is stored.

Six doors, one path, all live today. An Agentforce agent written in Agent Script decides what to say. A deterministic layer decides what may be true: it ranks the library, strips conditions, and holds the consent gate. So the parts that keep somebody safe never depend on a model remembering a rule.
