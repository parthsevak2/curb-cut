Curb Cut: an Agentforce agent that's on the worker's side.

PROBLEM TO SOLVE

Thirty in a hundred white-collar workers have a disability. Three tell their employer. The rest do the maths every day: what would it cost to ask for the chair, the lamp, the agenda, the hour? Who would I have to tell, and what would they think of me on Monday? Most decide it isn't worth it, and carry on hurting quietly.

The form is the barrier. To get help you must find the policy, learn its words, hold a work login, and explain your body, in writing, to the person who decides what you're worth. For a contractor, a temp, someone who is Deaf, someone whose first language isn't English, or someone with a condition nobody can see, that form is a door that doesn't open.

The money was never the barrier. Of the 1,425 employers who gave the Job Accommodation Network cost figures through 2024, 61% said the accommodation cost them nothing. The asking was.

OUR SOLUTION

Curb Cut turns asking into one sentence. You say what's hard, in your own words, and you never have to say why. It shows you what other people in your situation have asked for, from a library of real accommodations, each with its source and what it usually costs. Knowing is allowed, and asking is optional. If you do want to ask, it writes the request in your words, reads it back, and sends nothing until you clearly say yes.

There's no field for a diagnosis anywhere. It isn't encrypted or hidden behind a permission. It simply isn't there, and a build check fails if anyone adds one. Name a condition anyway and it's stripped before anything is stored, and you're told so.

Six doors, one path, all live today. An Agentforce agent written in Agent Script decides what to say. A deterministic layer decides what may be true: it ranks the library, strips conditions, and holds the consent gate. So the answer is the same whichever door you use, and the parts that keep somebody safe never depend on a model remembering a rule.
- Web, no login, no account, for the people outside the system.
- Text CURB CUT to +1 276 495 9311, because a basic phone is still a phone.
- Voice on the same number, for anyone who can't type. It never rings you back.
- Email, for the person with only a personal address.
- Slack, private message only. It says up front that Slack belongs to your employer, and never posts to a channel.
- Any assistant you already use, through tools that can find and draft but never send.

Ask once, not forever. A standing preference in your words travels ahead of you to the people you choose. WHO shows you who has seen it. OFF withdraws it, no reason asked.

It may look like a heavy build. It isn't. Agentforce made the ambitious part ordinary. What it took was intention: building for the person on the wrong side of the form, and saying no to a diagnosis field even when it was the easy default.
