Every workplace runs on unasked questions

The parent who needs to leave at four for pickup. The expecting mom who could use a little logistical grace. The new hire who'd do better work with a bigger monitor, and never says so, because asking means justifying yourself to the person who decides your career.

The cost lands hardest on disabled workers. More than one in four US adults has a disability, yet at a typical large employer only 3.5% of staff have told their employer about one. And of employers who told the Job Accommodation Network what an accommodation cost, 61% said it cost nothing.

Money is rarely the barrier. The hard part is the asking.

What it does

For the person

Say what's hard in one sentence. See what others have asked for, with usual costs. If you want to ask, it writes the request in your words, and nothing is sent until you clearly say yes.

For the organisation

Every ask arrives as a structured record in Salesforce with a committed answer-by date and an audit trail, instead of a hallway remark that evaporates. One volunteer on the access desk answers.

For the person with no login

A cleaner, a picker, a contractor mid-shift can text or call from the most basic phone. No internet, no app, no account. The number lives on a break-room poster.

  • No diagnosis field, anywhere. The build fails if anyone adds one. A field that doesn't exist can't be leaked, shown to a manager, or subpoenaed.
  • The agent talks; the code decides. Agentforce holds the conversation. What's stored and what's sent is decided by deterministic Apex, checked on every build.
  • Every door ends at a human. Say "human" on any channel and a person on the access desk picks it up.

Wheelchair users fought for curb cuts, and now everyone rolls through them: strollers, suitcases, delivery carts. Build for the people who get left out, and it gets better for everyone.

Bring it to your organisation

The code is free under Apache 2.0 and installs into the Salesforce org you already run. Web and email work with nothing else; text and voice need your own Twilio number and a small relay; Slack installs from a manifest in two minutes. Four settings carry your number, site and mailbox.

What it truly needs is one volunteer willing to be the person on the other end. Technical skill is secondary. If only one thing can be asked of an organisation, it's willingness.

No technical team? Still yours.

If you run a nonprofit, or your plate is simply full, write to curbcut@havihi.digital and we'll walk the setup in with you. For a good cause, the help is free too.
